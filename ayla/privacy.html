Privacy Policy

What data Ayla processes, where it is stored, and what it never does.

Last updated: 28 September 2026

In short. Ayla has no server and does not even hold the internet permission. Your period records, symptoms and notes are stored only on your phone, encrypted. Nothing is sent to us, because the app is technically unable to send data anywhere.

No account, no email, no ads, no analytics, no crash reporting, no cloud backup.

Your data leaves your phone only when you export it yourself.

1. What data we process

Ayla processes only the data needed to run the app. You create all of it, and all of it stays on your phone:

DataContents and purpose
Period recordsThe start and end dates you mark. Used for cycle calculations and predictions
Daily logsFlow, mood, energy level, pain level, symptoms, hours of sleep and a short note for a given day
Intercourse recordsThe days you mark. Used to produce a per-cycle summary for people planning a pregnancy
Cycle settingsThe average cycle length and period length you entered during setup
App settingsYour theme and reminder preferences
App lockIf you set a PIN: not the PIN itself, but an irreversible hash of it (PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 with a random salt) and a failed-attempt counter

Data we do not collect: name, email address, phone number, date of birth, location, contacts, photos, device identifier, advertising ID, IP address logs, usage analytics, crash reports. The app asks for none of these, and even if it did, there is nowhere for it to send them.

If you email us (see Contact) we will of course see your email address and what you wrote. We use it only to reply to you.

2. We know this is health data

Menstrual cycles, symptoms and intercourse records are special category personal data. The safest way to handle such data is not to collect it at all. Ayla is built on that principle: because the data never leaves your device, no copy ever exists that we could access, leak, be compelled to hand over, or lose in a breach.

We hold no record of your data. This is not a promise, it is a consequence of how the app is built.

3. Where your data is stored

On your phone, encrypted

All of your records live in a database inside the app's own private storage area, which other apps on Android cannot reach.

On top of that, the database file is encrypted with SQLCipher. The encryption key is held in your device's hardware-backed secure store (Android Keystore) and cannot be extracted from it. Someone who copies the database file off your phone cannot read its contents.

There is a trade-off you should know about: because the key is bound to the device, uninstalling the app or clearing its data makes your records permanently unrecoverable. Exporting a backup from time to time is a good habit.

Excluded from cloud backup

Android can back up app data to your Google account at the operating-system level. Ayla turns this off (allowBackup="false"). Your health records are not copied to Google Drive, to cloud backup, or automatically to a new phone when you switch devices. To move them yourself, use the export file described below.

Backup files you save yourself

Profile → Data Management → Export data saves all of your records as a JSON file to a location you choose. Android's own file picker asks where to put it; the app never writes it somewhere on your behalf.

This file is not encrypted. The in-app encryption does not cover it: it contains your period dates, symptoms and notes in plain text. From the moment it is saved it is entirely your responsibility — anyone you share it with can read it, and uploading it to a cloud service hands it to that provider. Keep it somewhere safe.

Import data restores such a file. Importing replaces your existing records, so the app asks you to confirm.

4. App lock and biometrics

You can optionally lock the app with a PIN.

  • Your PIN is not stored. Only an irreversible hash is kept (PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256, 120,000 rounds, random salt). The PIN cannot be recovered from that hash.
  • Repeated wrong entries progressively slow down further attempts.
  • While the lock is enabled, the app blocks screenshots and hides its content from Android's recent-apps preview.
  • If you choose fingerprint or face unlock, Android performs the verification entirely. The app never has access to your biometric data, never sees it and never stores it; it only receives a "verified" answer from Android.

If you forget your PIN there is no way to unlock the app. Not for us either. You would have to reset the app from your phone's settings, which erases your records. Exporting a backup before enabling the lock is recommended.

5. Permissions used

PermissionWhy it is needed
NotificationsTo show period-approaching and daily reminders you have chosen. You may decline it; the app works fully without notifications

That is the complete list of permissions Ayla requests. In particular:

There is no internet permission. The app's permission list does not contain android.permission.INTERNET. This is stronger than a promise not to send data: even if the app tried, the operating system would not let it reach the network. You can verify this yourself in your phone's app info screen.

Location, camera, microphone, contacts, photos, calendar, health platform and advertising ID permissions are not requested either.

6. Data sharing

Ayla does not share, sell or rent your data to anyone. There is no exchange of data with any ad network, analytics provider, data broker or other third party.

The app contains no third-party advertising, analytics, tracking or crash-reporting library. Without the internet permission, it connects to no external service at all.

7. What appears on your screen and in notifications

While your phone is locked, reminder notifications show only a generic line on the lock screen ("Ayla – You have a new reminder"). Your period dates, symptoms or any other health detail never appear there. You see the reminder itself once you unlock the phone.

8. Medical disclaimer

Ayla is not a diagnostic or treatment application. Its predictions are approximations based on the history you recorded and are not a substitute for medical advice.

Fertility predictions must not be used as a method of contraception.

If the symptoms you record are unusual for you, or if you are concerned, consider consulting a healthcare professional.

9. Children's privacy

Ayla is not directed at children. Because it collects no age, identity or contact information, it does not process children's personal data either.

10. Deleting your data

All of your data is under your control:

  • Individually: you can edit and delete daily logs and period marks from within the app.
  • Everything: Profile → Data Management → Delete all data permanently erases your records.
  • Uninstalling the app also deletes all data on your phone. "Clear storage" in Android settings does the same.
  • Backup files: you delete the files you saved yourself; only you know where they are.

Since we hold no copy, there is no deletion request to send us; we have nothing to delete. If you want emails you sent us deleted, just ask.

11. Your rights

Regulations such as the GDPR and Turkey's KVKK grant you rights to access, rectify, erase and port your data. In Ayla all of these are exercised directly inside the app, without asking anyone's permission: you see your records on screen, edit them, delete them, and take them with you as a machine-readable file via Export data.

There is no situation in which you would need to contact us, because we process none of your data.
